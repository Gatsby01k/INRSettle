# INRSettle — Permanent positioning

Canonical product identity for the website, LinkedIn, metadata, documentation
and investor materials. Written to remain unchanged for years: no beta status,
no provider names, no corridor details, no feature lists that will grow stale,
no claims the product cannot defend indefinitely.

---

## 1. Tagline

**Proof of settlement.**

## 2. One-sentence positioning

**INRSettle verifies settlement of INR payouts made through third-party
providers — recorded approval, provider proof, independent bank
reconciliation and an audit trail for every payment — without moving funds.**

## 3. LinkedIn headline

**Settlement verification for INR payouts. Provider proof, independent
reconciliation, audit trail. We do not move funds.**

## 4. Company About

When a payout provider marks a payment completed, that is a claim, not proof.
The money may have arrived. The record of it — who approved the payment, what
the provider reported, whether a bank statement confirms it — usually lives in
spreadsheets and message threads, if it exists at all.

INRSettle is the control layer for INR payouts made through providers. Every settlement
carries a recorded approval, the provider's own execution record, an
independent match against bank or PSP records, and an append-only audit
trail. The result is a report per settlement that finance teams, auditors and
counterparties can rely on. A provider's status alone never makes a payment
final; independent evidence does.

INRSettle does not move, custody, or provide funds. Providers move money;
INRSettle proves what happened. It is built for the teams accountable for
payouts they do not execute themselves: payout operators, payment providers,
treasury desks, and compliance and risk functions.

## 5. One-paragraph manifesto

Payment execution has been automated for decades; payment verification has
not. Every company that pays out through providers runs on a quiet asymmetry:
the provider holds the systems, the records and the version of events, while
the company holds the liability. "Completed" is accepted as final because
checking is expensive — until a payment is questioned, and a spreadsheet is
the only defense. We build the other half of the payment stack: the record
of approval, proof, reconciliation and finality that exists outside the
provider's control. Settlement finality should be something you prove, not
something you are told. That is the entire product, and it will still be the
entire product in ten years.

## Usage notes

- The tagline pairs with the logo and stands alone; it is not a sentence and
  takes no elaboration.
- The one-sentence positioning is the meta description, the investor
  one-liner, and the first sentence anywhere a single sentence is allowed.
- The thesis line "Payment completed ≠ settlement finalized." remains the
  website headline (hero + report footer). It is rhetoric built on this
  positioning, not a replacement for it.
